7.1 Category Establishment

  • A “Projects” List
  • Project support material
  • Calendar actions and information
  • A “Next Actions” List
  • A “Waiting For” List
  • Reference material
  • A “Someday/Maybe” List

Note: Lists don’t need to be assigned priorities.

7.2 Organizing Action Reminders

7.2.1 Day- or Time-Specific Actions

When you make any appointment, write it into your calendar and trust your calendar.

7.2.2 ASAP Actions

Categorize these actions by context. Some examples include:

  • By Tool
    • “At Computer”
    • “Errand” (i.e., need a car)
  • By Person
    • “Boss”
    • “Partner”
    • “Your Attorney”
  • By Meeting
    • “Staff Meeting”
    • “Project Meeting”
  • By Place
    • “At Home”
    • “At Office”

7.3 Organizing “Waiting For”

Keep the “Waiting For” list close at hand.

  • It is a trigger about what you are waiting for from whom

7.4 Organizing Projects Reminders

Create and maintain the “Projects” List

  • Personal projects
  • Professional projects
  • Delegated projects

7.5 Organizing Project Support Materials

Do NOT use support material for reminding

7.6 Organizing Ad Hoc Thinking

Use attached notes, Email, database, paper-based files, or notebooks

7.7 Organizagin Non-Actionable Data

7.7.1 Reference System

  • Install one General-Reference Filing (and only one)
  • Install a separate Large-Category Filing for any topic that requires more then 50 file folders
  • Use Rolodexes for your network
    • Home and office phone numbers, addresses, cell-phone numbers, fax numbers, email addresses
    • Birthdays, names of friends’ and colleagues family members, hobbies, favorite wines and foods, etc.
  • Build a Library and Archives for things you think will need in the meetings, trips, office, etc.

7.7.2 “Someday/Maybe” List

  • Install your own tickler system
    • Do NOT share it with others
  • Update it every day
  • Make an inventory of your creative imaginings:
    • Things to get or build for your home
    • Hobbies to take up
    • Skills to learn
    • Creative expression to explore
    • Clothes and accessories to buy
    • Toys and gears to acquire
    • Trips to take
    • Organizations to join
    • Service projects to contribute to
    • Things to see and do
  • Categories can include:
    • Food: Recipes, menus, restaurants, wines
    • Family: Things to do with them
    • Books
    • CDs
    • Videos
    • Cultural events
    • Gift ideas
    • Garden ideas
    • Websites to surf
    • Weekend trips
    • Meeting ideas
    • Party ideas

7.8 Organizing Checklists

7.8.1 How/Why Do You Need Checklists?

  • The need for checklists depends on your familiarity to the tasks.
  • The more novel the situation, the more control (checklists) is required.

7.8.2 When Do You Need Checlists?

A two-dimensional method:

  • By Areas of Commitments (i.e., work)
    • Career goals
    • Service
    • Family
    • Relationships
    • Community
    • Heath and energy
    • Financial resources
    • Creative expression
  • By Areas of Responsibility
    • Team morale
    • Processes
    • Timelines
    • Staff issues
    • Workload
    • Communication

7.8.3 Develop Checklists at All Levels

  • Personal Affirmations
    • Personal value statements
  • Job Area of Responsibility
  • Travel
    • Everything to take on or do before a trip
  • Weekly Review
    • Everything to review and/or update on a weekly basis
  • Training Program Components
    • All things to handle when putting on an event, front to back
  • Conference Checklist
    • Everything to handle when putting on a conference
  • Focus Areas
    • Key life roles and responsibilities
  • Key People in My Life/Work
    • Relationships to assess regularly for completion and opportunity development
  • Organization Chart
    • Key people and areas of output to manage and maintain
  • Personal Development
    • Things to evaluate regularly to ensure personal balance and progress
